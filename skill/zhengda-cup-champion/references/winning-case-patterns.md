# Winning-case patterns

## Use cases as evidence, not templates

Study successful projects to learn mechanisms. Do not copy titles, questionnaires, data, narratives, or recommendations. Preserve source and edition because judging trends change.

Do not infer an award from polish, filenames, or self-description. Record award provenance explicitly as `official_verified`, `user_attested`, `artifact_claimed`, or `unknown`, and keep the exact prize, edition, group, region, track, and stage separate.

The three cases in `case-corpus.md` are `user_attested` national first- or second-prize works. The exact first/second-prize mapping remains unknown. Use them as an award-corpus pattern set and a reuse-safety corpus at the same time: infer why the work is memorable and decision-ready, while independently testing every method, number, privacy boundary, and frozen deliverable. Without judge feedback or a non-award comparison group, label these mechanisms `analyst_inference`; do not present co-occurrence as award causation.

## Observed recurring patterns

1. **A hook plus narrowing.** The title creates recall; the full title narrows the population and object and may also state geography or decision context. Require the problem statement to finish any missing operational boundary.
2. **A decision tension, not only a topic.** The work states who must decide what and names the consequential friction that makes new evidence necessary.
3. **The reported architecture places exploration before the instrument.** Literature, stakeholder interviews, observation, or social listening is presented as supplying respondent language, constructs, and questionnaire modules. Treat this as `3_of_3_reported_architectures`, not verified chronology; one supplied case has a disclosed date conflict.
4. **Fieldwork process disclosure is visible.** Recruitment, pilot, questionnaire changes, achieved sample, quality control, exclusions, and limitations are inspectable in the artifacts. This does not independently authenticate fieldwork or data.
5. **Two-axis segmentation.** Strong cases commonly combine a population or lifecycle split with a context, category, region, or service split.
6. **An analysis ladder.** Description, difference/driver analysis, segmentation, mechanism/priority analysis, and action form a cumulative sequence. Each method has one narrative job.
7. **One mother insight.** In the three reports, a small number of named, non-obvious conclusions integrate the models and link findings to actions. Check report-to-deck stability where a deck exists; cross-defense stability is a normative target because no defense artifacts were supplied.
8. **Finding-action correspondence.** Most headline findings and segments have a clear recommendation counterpart; audit the remaining coverage instead of assuming perfect isomorphism.
9. **Report/deck role separation.** In the two supplied decks, the report carries the complete proof burden while the deck reorders the material into a decision story and spends the largest block on differentiated findings. This describes artifact roles, not a finding that every supplied analysis is already proven.
10. **Memorable visual identity.** The three reports, and the two supplied decks where available, use a topic-specific grammar without displacing evidence.

## Normative enhancements added by the Agent

1. **Implementation completeness.** Require recommendations to name owner, action, sequence, resources, KPI, risk, pilot, and stop rule. The supplied cases do not consistently evidence every field; this is an improvement target, not a 3/3 commonality.
2. **Defense ownership.** Require team members to explain data provenance, exclusions, assumptions, segment logic, action trade-offs, and the exact evidence behind headline visuals. No defense artifacts were supplied, so this is a competition-readiness target rather than an observed case pattern.

## Dual-ledger extraction

For every award case, maintain two parallel records:

- **award-corpus pattern:** what appears to create decision value, recall, coherence, credibility, and actionability, labeled as analyst inference rather than award cause;
- **reuse safety:** what would fail current method, inference, reproducibility, privacy, rights, anonymity, or frozen-submission gates.

Never average these ledgers into a single vague score. A real award outcome is evidence that the complete work succeeded in its field and stage; it is not proof that every table, model, code fragment, or metadata layer is safe to reproduce.

## Report-to-deck transformation

Before drafting a deck from a report:

1. freeze the canonical fact and model registries;
2. identify the decision tension and one mother insight;
3. select the smallest set of findings needed to prove that insight;
4. map each selected finding to an action or remove it from the main deck;
5. give each slide one job: claim -> evidence -> implication;
6. compress routine literature and validity checks without hiding material limitations;
7. reserve explicit closing space for named conclusions and action architecture;
8. place detailed evidence, robustness, and reproducibility material in the report or appendix, not in unreadable slide miniatures.

In the two supplied national-prize decks, differentiated findings occupy the largest content block. Treat this as a mechanism to test, not a fixed slide-count formula.

## Case extraction schema

For each case record:

- edition, group, stage, award, award provenance, source, and verification status;
- decision problem and beneficiary;
- population, geography, sample, and fieldwork;
- qualitative and quantitative methods;
- strongest finding and evidence strength;
- recommendation mechanism;
- visual/narrative device;
- award-corpus pattern, inference status, evidence base, reuse-safety limitation, and transferable pattern.

Do not infer private judge reasoning. Label analytical interpretations as inference.

## Differentiation test

Require at least two substantive differentiators:

- a neglected population or decision context;
- a newly important mechanism;
- better access or stronger fieldwork;
- a distinctive behavioral, experimental, spatial, or longitudinal design;
- an implementation partner or measurable pilot;
- a superior evidence-to-action loop.

Cosmetic title changes and additional models do not count.
