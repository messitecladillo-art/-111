# User-provided case corpus

## Status and use boundary

This corpus contains abstracted lessons from three user-provided Zhengda Cup works. The user has explicitly identified all three as national first- or second-prize works. Their award provenance is therefore `user_attested`; the exact first/second-prize mapping and official award evidence remain `unknown` rather than independently verified.

- Treat the cases as positive evidence of patterns that co-occur in these award-level artifacts, while maintaining a second ledger for method, reproducibility, privacy, and frozen-submission defects. Without judge feedback or a comparison group, these patterns are analyst inferences, not causal explanations of the awards.
- The files themselves claim or imply the sixteenth edition, but edition, group, region, track, stage, commission, and exact award mapping have not been independently verified.
- A filename containing “国赛” is not independent evidence; the award classification comes from the user's attestation.
- The source binaries remain outside the repository. Their reuse and retention rights are `unknown`; only hashes, structural facts, locators, and abstracted lessons are recorded.
- Do not copy titles, slogans, questionnaire wording, respondent material, code, numbers, photographs, illustrations, IP, or recommendations.
- Internal diagnostics below test whether a mechanism is safe to reuse. They are not official judging results and do not negate the user-attested award outcome.

The machine provenance record is `corpus-source/case-studies/user-case-intake-2026-08-04.json`; the normalized cross-case mechanism set is `corpus-source/case-studies/national-award-patterns-2026-08-04.json`.

## Cross-case patterns in the award corpus

The following patterns are present in all three reports unless marked otherwise. They are `analyst_inference` from a three-case positive corpus, not identified award causes. PPT claims are based on the two supplied decks; the new-blue-collar case has no supplied deck. No defense artifacts were supplied.

1. **A memorable hook is paired with a narrowing subtitle.** Wordplay or a cultural metaphor earns attention, while the full title clarifies the population and object and, where present, geography or decision context. The operational boundary must still be fixed in the problem statement.
2. **The topic contains a productive tension.** Dual-track identity versus weak integration, degree status versus new-blue-collar work, and emotional demand versus participation friction each create a question that cannot be answered by simple description.
3. **The scope is deliberately bounded.** Each work defines a local venue, a specific labor group, or a named consumption domain instead of treating a broad social trend as the survey object.
4. **The reported architecture places exploration before formal analysis.** Literature plus interviews or social-listening work is presented as discovering respondent language, constructs, stakeholders, and questionnaire modules. This is not proof of chronology: the new-blue-collar report discloses pilot dates earlier than the interviews it says informed the questionnaire, so the Agent must verify dated evidence.
5. **Pilot and fieldwork process disclosure is visible.** The works expose pilot size, instrument changes or checks, achieved samples, data-quality procedures, and fieldwork artifacts rather than hiding the research process. Visibility is not independent authentication of the fieldwork or data.
6. **Segmentation has at least two meaningful axes.** Examples include student/resident and viewing status, platform/skill work and latent profiles, and experience status plus entertainment category/profile. Findings therefore answer “for whom” and “under what situation.”
7. **Analysis advances as a ladder.** The recurring functional sequence is baseline description -> difference or driver analysis -> segment/profile discovery -> mechanism or priority analysis -> action. Model names vary and are not the pattern to copy.
8. **One mother insight unifies the report.** The best case-level conclusion is not a list of model outputs. It is a reusable explanatory idea that links findings, conclusions, and actions: a connector group, a stable profile system, or a lifecycle friction. Cross-deck stability is observed only in the two supplied decks; defense stability is a target to test, not an observed case property.
9. **Conclusions and recommendations have close structural correspondence.** Most headline conclusions become indexes for action packages. Person-level, category-level, and system-level findings lead to corresponding actions, while the Agent still audits orphan findings and unsupported actions explicitly.
10. **The report carries the proof burden while the deck persuades.** The two supplied decks devote the largest content block to differentiated analysis, rapidly compress routine literature and validity checks, then reserve several slides for named conclusions and actions. They do not miniaturize every report page. The proof burden is a role distinction, not a claim that every supplied analysis is already proven.
11. **Visual identity reinforces the research object.** Each case uses a topic-specific cover, palette, navigation device, and recurring motif. Report pages remain more formal; decks use more expressive pacing and section breaks.
12. **Research labor is made inspectable.** Questionnaires, interviews, field images, data excerpts, code, variable dictionaries, or AI disclosures appear in the report or appendices. Completeness is a credibility signal, although included code must still reproduce the claimed result.

### Observed report-to-deck compression

For the two supplied decks, excluding covers, agenda, section dividers, and closing slides:

- context and problem framing consume roughly 18-21% of content slides;
- research design and credibility consume roughly 11-17%;
- differentiated findings and analytical mechanisms consume roughly 42-53%;
- named conclusions and actions consume roughly 18-21%.

These ranges are case observations, not a mandatory template. The transferable rule is to allocate the largest share to non-obvious evidence and preserve enough time to close the evidence-to-action loop.

## Case card: Wuhan football dual-track event

### Evidence anchors

- Report: 320 rendered pages; DOCX SHA-256 `14c3d612adbdb57dada2fd0a183746719700d14a080d8d2e1efffdea6da181a9`.
- Deck: 26 slides; PPTX SHA-256 `7c45a1e0d3f525c5fe0e63479898e576529a2715cd8e0c1e89e6b72a01ca9ac1`.
- Core sample arithmetic is internally coherent: issued 679, valid 641, excluded 38, students 328, residents 313, and viewing-status groups 447 + 85 + 54 + 55 = 641 (report printed pp. 17–19; deck slides 8, 10, 11, 21).

### Transferable structure

- Start from a real management choice: whether and how to retain a city–university dual-track event design.
- Separate student and resident audiences, then map publicity, venue, schedule, and experience actions to different operators.
- Use a short closing sequence of findings followed by owner-oriented actions.
- Package questionnaire, interview material, data views, code, and AI disclosure instead of delivering prose alone.

### Do not learn these shortcuts

- Terminal intercept and convenience recruitment makes the full design non-probability even when upper stages use strata or PPS language. A simple-random-sample formula cannot be called the “best sample size” for this achieved design (report pp. 12–14; deck slide 8).
- Four nominal viewing categories do not acquire a natural order merely because they are coded 1–4; direct Spearman analysis is invalid without a defensible ordering (report pp. 23–24; deck slide 12).
- A silhouette value near 0.24 does not support “clear boundaries,” and appendix code that never calculates the claimed selection statistic cannot reproduce the chosen K (report pp. 31–32; appendix pp. 259–262; deck slide 14).
- An outcome mechanically constructed from the same satisfaction items cannot be regressed on those items and relabeled as “true driver weights” (report pp. 35–38; deck slide 16).
- AHP consistency does not prove real RMB cost. Hard-coded matrices without expert provenance, quotes, labor hours, or sensitivity cannot support claims such as budget reduction (report pp. 41–46 and appendix pp. 294–295; deck slides 18–19).
- Self-reported interest filters are not realized conversion rates. Any 12%, 20%, 25%, 30%, or 40% operating gain needs a baseline, parameter source, uncertainty, and pilot label (report pp. 51, 54–57).
- Report and deck disagree on social-platform scope and sentiment framing; the canonical source registry must precede narrative compression (report pp. 5, 12, 53; deck slides 5–6).

## Case card: Wuhan graduate new-blue-collar employment

### Evidence anchors

- Frozen PDF: 95 pages; SHA-256 `fa3c4291924fb68ea16a550c4c2cd8463fb8b1892346382d8c076eba3aae6300`.
- Editable DOCX: 97 pages under the intake renderer; SHA-256 `65c6efba92816b25a81922aa31047a00dae96bb0c1bb8ea1c86522c34863d30f`.
- Normalized-text similarity is about 0.989 and the numeric token sets match, but pagination differs. The PDF is the more stable visual baseline; the artifacts are not interchangeable frozen versions.
- The report self-describes 12 interviews and 821 valid questionnaires. These counts are not independently verified because raw data, exclusion logs, and a bound reproducible environment were not provided.

### Transferable structure

- Define a bounded target population before fieldwork and explicitly acknowledge when individual recruitment is non-probability (PDF pp. 18–25).
- Record pilot changes rather than reporting only alpha, KMO, or Bartlett results (PDF pp. 22–23).
- Build differentiated action packages around profiles and needs instead of offering one average policy bundle (PDF pp. 40–64).
- Use a coherent editorial system and disclose AI assistance, while keeping disclosure tied to a complete ledger (PDF pp. 79–80).

### Do not learn these shortcuts

- Questionnaire, variable dictionary, raw values, code mappings, tables, and prose must form a bijection. Here, Q3/Q4 semantics, actual versus expected income, target-eligibility logic, and option counts do not align (PDF pp. 66–73, 91–93).
- Qualitative work cannot be credited with shaping a questionnaire if its disclosed field dates occur after the pilot and formal launch, absent a separate documented discovery stage (PDF pp. 20–22, 26).
- A KANO category must follow the recorded response matrix and modal rule. The skill-training item is labeled O in some locations and A in another despite frequencies favoring O (PDF pp. 2, 33, 35, 60).
- LPA claims require the real model data and fit diagnostics. Simulated n=800 K-means-style code cannot reproduce an n=821 latent-profile analysis reporting AIC/BIC/Entropy/AvePP (PDF pp. 40–42, 85–87).
- The named model must match the implementation. `GradientBoostingClassifier` is not XGBoost, and a four-feature full-sample fit without holdout metrics cannot generate or validate the ten-feature SHAP narrative (PDF pp. 51–52, 87–89).
- Significance in one subgroup and non-significance in another does not prove a between-group difference. Measurement invariance and formal coefficient-difference tests must precede group claims (PDF pp. 49–55).
- A questionnaire bin boundary of 5,000 yuan is not an empirical threshold without a prespecified continuous interaction, change-point model, or sensitivity analysis (PDF pp. 54–55).
- Pseudonym plus age, university, job, tenure, face, local absolute path, and document metadata can re-identify people or teams. Privacy and anonymity scanning must include visible and binary layers (PDF pp. 76–77, 87–90).

## Case card: Generation-Z offline entertainment

### Evidence anchors

- Report: 65 pages; SHA-256 `20bc459f9b2e45c3f33019fe2ad9ef3b007bd8733107e535316291c7cd55f75f`.
- Deck: 45 slides; SHA-256 `a61de53d5dfb60744d1994db4610c2d656c5d5fe412842582f184829208d2e5d`.
- The report/deck state 206 received, 204 valid, and a 99.3% valid rate. Direct calculation gives 204/206 = 99.029%, which rounds to 99.0% at one decimal, not 99.3% (report p. 19; deck slide 15).
- The report states 206 received and 204 valid responses. About 96.6% are target age, implying roughly 197; it separately states 137 experienced respondents, while three reported clusters sum to 197 (report pp. 19, 31–32, 39–41; deck slides 15, 22, 27).

### Transferable structure

- Use non-participant/participant as the observed experience split, then cross it with category and the three reported profiles.
- Combine category, peak experience, pain point, repeat intent, and word of mouth as an analysis map. A non-participant → participant → repeat participant → advocate journey is an analyst abstraction to test, not an observed four-stage segmentation in the source.
- Preserve a consistent report/deck visual language and distinguish entertainment categories before recommending actions.

### Do not learn these shortcuts

- Convenience plus quota sampling cannot support a simple-random 95% margin of error or nationwide Generation-Z inference (report pp. 12–15; deck slides 12–13).
- Every module needs an explicit denominator. Values based on 204, about 197, or 137 respondents cannot silently coexist; cluster counts must sum to the declared model sample.
- Effective rate must be calculated from its recorded count pair: 204/206 is 99.0% to one decimal, so the displayed 99.3% fails arithmetic review.
- 69.5% is not “more than seventy percent.” Narrative rounding must be mechanically checked (report pp. 2, 32, 62; deck slides 22, 40).
- Mean differences between people who mention or do not mention an attribute are not standard KANO without paired functional/dysfunctional questions and an evaluation matrix (report pp. 46–54; deck slides 30–31).
- A negative correlation cannot establish an inverted-U, and stratified significance patterns cannot establish mediation or “complete explanation” (report pp. 29–30; deck slides 19–20).
- A “machine-learning” income uplift needs an algorithm, target, train/test split, performance, calibration, code, and uncertainty. Setting all pains to zero or assuming 20%/30% conversion is a scenario, not prediction or validation (report pp. 55–58; deck slides 32–36).
- External exact numbers, scraped-platform identity, and social-listening method must be located and consistent across report and deck (report p. 59; deck slide 8).

## Cross-case rules now enforced by the Agent

### Award-corpus pattern ledger

1. **Hook plus boundary.** A memorable title receives credit only when the full title narrows the object and population and the problem statement fixes the remaining geography, scope, and decision owner.
2. **Tension before method.** State the consequential contradiction or friction before selecting an analysis technique.
3. **Two-axis segmentation.** Test at least one meaningful population/lifecycle split and one context/category split when the evidence supports them; do not force arbitrary personas.
4. **Analysis ladder.** Assign every method one narrative job and an explicit upstream input and downstream decision use.
5. **Mother-insight test.** Require a concise explanatory idea that integrates the strongest findings without overstating evidence.
6. **Finding-action coverage.** Every action package must map to a named finding or segment; every headline finding must either change a decision or be removed from the deck.
7. **Report/deck role separation.** The report is responsible for the full evidence chain. The deck reallocates attention toward differentiated findings and actions instead of shrinking the table of contents.
8. **Dual coding.** Record the candidate mechanisms that make an award-level artifact decision-ready and why a specific device may still be unsafe to reuse. Label causal interpretation as unverified; award outcome never overrides method, privacy, or compliance checks.

### Reuse-safety ledger

1. **Scope before prestige.** Record edition, group, region, track, stage, award, source, and verification status. Never infer an award from packaging.
2. **Artifact fingerprint before review.** Bind every source and final output to path, version, SHA-256, bytes, and page/slide count. A new export invalidates prior visual and anonymity approval.
3. **One quantitative fact source.** `evidence/evidence-ledger.csv` records population, analysis sample, numerator, denominator, unit, reported value, evidence, limitation, and every output location.
4. **Arithmetic before prose.** Recalculate percentages, subgroup sums, cluster sizes, effective rates, uplift formulas, and rounding before accepting headline language.
5. **Method name as contract.** KANO, K-means/LPA, SEM, mediation, nonlinear relationships, AHP/IPA, text analysis, and predictive models must meet the minimum disclosures in `methods-router.md`.
6. **Implementation identity.** Model name, library class, data object, features, sample, code, output, and chart must match. Simulated demonstration code can never serve as reproduction evidence.
7. **Actual, inferred, simulated, proposed.** These classes remain separate in the ledger and in prose. Scenarios cannot be called validated, predicted-realized, or actual impact.
8. **Questionnaire-to-output bijection.** Eligibility logic, item wording, option codes, variable dictionary, data columns, transformations, tables, and claims must resolve one-to-one.
9. **Cross-artifact currentness.** Report, PDF, deck, abstract, script, appendix, and data package share one fact set and one model registry. Silent compression or renaming fails review.
10. **Binary plus visual anonymity.** Scan physical archive boundaries, filenames, metadata, image EXIF/XMP/IPTC/GPS, custom properties, comments, revisions, notes, hidden slides, active relationships, embedded objects, PDF xref/attachments/thumbnails/actions, paths, faces, screenshots, alt text, and visible content.
11. **Evidence-to-action card.** Each final action names evidence IDs, decision owner, responsible owner, resource or cost basis, timing, KPI baseline and target, risk, pilot, and stop rule.
12. **Reproducibility cap.** Missing raw data, exclusion log, bound code, model outputs, or environment is missing evidence, not proof of fabrication; it still caps the confidence and readiness score.

## Regression expectations derived from this corpus

At minimum, evaluations must reject:

- a non-probability sample paired with a simple-random margin of error or population wording;
- a percentage that does not equal its numerator and denominator, or language such as “over 70%” for 69.5%;
- an effective-rate claim of 99.3% when the registered counts are 204/206 and therefore round to 99.0%;
- clusters/classes whose counts do not sum to the declared model sample;
- KANO without paired functional/dysfunctional items and its matrix;
- an inverted-U from a signed correlation, or mediation from subgroup comparisons;
- a model name that differs from the executed implementation;
- simulated or unbound example code offered as reproduction of real results;
- predictive claims without held-out validation and performance/calibration evidence;
- subgroup-difference claims based only on one significant and one non-significant coefficient;
- a bin edge presented as a behavioral threshold without threshold analysis;
- questionnaire options, dictionary codes, and table categories that cannot be mapped;
- report/PPT disagreement on sample, platform, method, model, or result;
- anonymous output with identifying metadata, review residue, hidden content, or local absolute paths;
- a frozen PDF and editable source being treated as the same approved version after pagination drift.

Use these cases both to generate positive mechanism tests and to generate adversarial tests and repair priorities. Do not copy their protected expression, reported data, questionnaire wording, or visual assets, and do not treat any individual method implementation as correct merely because the work received an award.
